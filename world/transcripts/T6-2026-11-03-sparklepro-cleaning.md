# Call transcript — SparklePro Cleaning — 2026-11-03

Participants: Alex Chen (Account Executive, Shiftpoint), Kevin O'Brien (COO, SparklePro Cleaning), Lisa Tran (Branch Operations Lead, SparklePro Cleaning)

**Alex Chen:** Morning, Kevin. Morning, Lisa. Thanks for moving things around for this.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Morning, Alex. Sorry about last week, I had a client walk-through I couldn't move.

**Lisa Tran:** Good morning!

**Alex Chen:** Totally fine. So, quick recap from our discovery call, and then I'll show you the product. Kevin, correct me if anything's changed.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Sure. Fourteen branches, about six hundred fifty cleaners, almost all nights. We clean offices, schools, medical buildings — around four hundred buildings a night.

**Alex Chen:** And the big numbers were about six percent of shifts going uncovered at short notice, around ninety thousand in client credits last year for missed cleans, and branch leads spending about six hours a week each on rotas and calling around.

**Kevin O'Brien:** That's it. And supervisors driving to buildings at ten p.m. to see if anybody showed up. That's the one that drives me nuts.

**Alex Chen:** Okay, so that's where I'll start. Let me share my screen. This is the manager view, and I've set it up as if it were your Omaha Central branch. Each building is a site under the branch, and the shifts are the nightly cleans.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Okay.

**Alex Chen:** Here's the open-shift flow. Say a cleaner at the Lincoln North branch calls in sick at four in the afternoon. The branch lead clicks on that shift, hits "post as open shift", and everybody qualified at that branch gets a notification on their phone. First person to grab it, the lead approves, done.

**Kevin O'Brien:** And the lead doesn't have to call anybody.

**Alex Chen:** Doesn't have to call anybody. And they can see right here which shifts are still uncovered for tonight, in red.

**Lisa Tran:** Can the cleaner see which building it is before they take it?

**Alex Chen:** Yes — the building, the address, the hours. Let me switch to the phone view so you see what they see. Here: "Open shift, tonight, 6 to 10 p.m., Midtown Medical Building." Tap "pick up".

**Lisa Tran:** Okay, that's clear. Is that in Spanish too? About half our crews prefer Spanish.

**Alex Chen:** Yes. Each person picks their language in the app, and Spanish is fully supported.

**Lisa Tran:** Great. And the phones — a lot of our people are on prepaid Androids, some of them pretty old.

**Alex Chen:** Older Android phones are supported. And if someone doesn't install the app at all, they still get shift reminders by text. They just can't pick up open shifts themselves.

**Lisa Tran:** That's fine. Most of them will use it if it gets them extra hours.

**Alex Chen:** That's usually what happens. Now, Kevin, the thing you said was the must-have: GPS clock-in. When the cleaner arrives at the building, they clock in on the phone, and the app checks they're actually at that address. If they try to clock in from home, the lead gets flagged.

**Kevin O'Brien:** So my supervisors can stop driving around at night.

**Alex Chen:** That's the idea. This board — live attendance for tonight, every building. Green, clocked in on site. Gray, not started yet. Red, should've started and nobody's there.

**Kevin O'Brien:** That red one is a client credit waiting to happen.

**Alex Chen:** Exactly — and you'd see it at six-fifteen, not the next morning when the client calls.

**Lisa Tran:** What about cleaners who cover two branches? Council Bluffs and Omaha Central share a lot of people.

**Alex Chen:** A person can belong to more than one branch. They'd see open shifts at both, and their hours roll up, so the leads can see if someone's heading into overtime across the two.

**Lisa Tran:** Oh, that's good. Right now we only find that out on payroll day.

**Kevin O'Brien:** How do the branch leads talk to the crews? Right now it's a group text per branch and it's chaos.

**Alex Chen:** There's messaging per location. A lead can send an announcement to the whole branch — "the school on Dodge is closed Friday, crews reassigned" — and everybody gets it in the app, or by text if they don't have it.

**Kevin O'Brien:** And people can't reply-all with thumbs up?

**Alex Chen:** [laughs] No reply-all. It's announcements.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Sold. [laughs] Just kidding. Partly.

**Lisa Tran:** One more — turnover is really high for us, like seventy percent a year. How fast can we add a new hire?

**Alex Chen:** The lead adds them with a name and phone number, they get a text invite, download the app, and they can see their shifts the same day. Removing someone who leaves is one click, and their future shifts go back to open.

**Lisa Tran:** That would save me a lot of time. We add five or ten people a week across the branches.

**Alex Chen:** That's exactly why we built it that way. A lot of our cleaning and security customers have the same churn.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Okay. I like this a lot. Lisa?

**Lisa Tran:** I like it too. How long does training take for the branch leads?

**Alex Chen:** For fourteen branches you'd have the onboarding package — that's setup, importing your people and buildings, and two training sessions. Most leads are comfortable after the first session.

**Lisa Tran:** Okay, good.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Alright. What's the damage?

**Alex Chen:** [laughs] Fair. For you it's Core — open shifts, swaps, GPS time clock, messaging. Core is a hundred fifty dollars per location per month, billed yearly. Fourteen branches, that's twenty-five thousand two hundred a year. And the onboarding package is three thousand one-time, which is required at ten locations and up.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Per branch, not per building.

**Alex Chen:** Per branch. So per location the way you organize it — the buildings live under the branch.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Okay. Is Crewlane cheaper? Somebody mentioned them at a conference.

**Alex Chen:** Usually a bit, but their app is weaker. And for you the app is the whole thing — if crews don't pick up open shifts on their phones, you're back to the phone tree.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Fair enough.

**Alex Chen:** And just to tie it back: if you're at ninety thousand a year in client credits, and this cuts the uncovered shifts even by a third, that's more than the subscription.

**Kevin O'Brien:** Yeah, I did that math in my head already. Look — send me the offer and let's walk through it next week. If it looks like this, I'm good.

**Alex Chen:** Great. I'll put together a written offer: Core for fourteen branches, twenty-five thousand two hundred a year, plus three thousand onboarding. When's good next week for a walkthrough?

**Kevin O'Brien:** Wednesday morning?
