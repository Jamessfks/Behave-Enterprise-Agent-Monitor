# Call transcript — Little Acorns Childcare — 2026-10-28

Participants: Alex Chen (Account Executive, Shiftpoint), Emily Carter (Executive Director, Little Acorns Childcare), Sam Wood (Center Operations Coordinator, Little Acorns Childcare)

**Alex Chen:** Hi Emily, hi Sam. Thanks for joining. Emily, good to see you again.

**Emily Carter:** Hi Alex. Thank you for doing this. I brought Sam, because Sam is the one who actually lives in the schedules.

**Sam Wood:** Hey. Yeah, I'm the floater guy. [laughs]

**Alex Chen:** Perfect, then you're exactly who I want to hear from. Emily, just to make sure I've got the picture right before I share my screen?

**Emily Carter:** Sure. Eleven centers, about two hundred ninety staff. Lead teachers, assistant teachers, floaters, and our cooks. Every classroom has to meet ratio every hour, so every sick call is a scramble.

**Alex Chen:** And today it's the paper grids per classroom, photographed and posted, and floaters by text.

**Sam Wood:** Before seven a.m., usually. My phone starts going at six-fifteen.

**Alex Chen:** And the directors spend about six hours a week each on the grids, overtime around eight percent, mostly floaters, and twice this year a classroom had to close for a morning.

**Emily Carter:** Twice. And that's the part that really hurts — for families, and for the teachers who feel responsible. Our teachers are the heart of this.

**Alex Chen:** Understood. Let me share. So this is the rota builder, and I set it up like your North Boulder center. Instead of departments I made each classroom a row group — Infants, Toddlers, Preschool, Pre-K — and then the kitchen.

**Emily Carter:** Oh, that's nice. That looks like our grid.

**Alex Chen:** That's on purpose. Each classroom has a template — the standard shifts and the minimum number of staff you need on each shift. So for the infant room you'd say, minimum two people from seven to nine, three from nine to three, and so on.

**Sam Wood:** And you set that once?

**Alex Chen:** Set it once, then every week you hit "copy last week" and adjust. If a shift in the template doesn't have enough people assigned, it shows up as unfilled, here, in orange.

**Sam Wood:** Okay.

**Alex Chen:** Now the floater piece. When someone calls in sick, the director marks that shift as open, and it goes out to the floaters and anyone qualified at nearby centers in the app. Sam, you'd see all open shifts across all eleven centers in one place.

**Sam Wood:** Instead of my texts. Okay, that I like.

**Alex Chen:** Then on Pro — which is what I'd recommend for you — there are the compliance alerts. Breaks, overtime. So if a teacher is about to go into overtime because she picked up a second shift, the director sees it before approving.

**Emily Carter:** That's important. A lot of overtime is just tired teachers saying yes.

**Alex Chen:** Right. And the third piece is payroll export. Approved hours go straight to payroll, so nobody retypes the grids at the end of the period.

**Emily Carter:** Monica will appreciate that. She's our finance and admin manager.

**Sam Wood:** Can I ask something? Can it tell me when a room is under ratio?

**Alex Chen:** Good question, and I want to be precise, because I don't want to oversell this. What Shiftpoint does is: you set the minimum staffing per shift in the template, and if a shift is unfilled — because someone called in sick and nobody picked it up — it flags it. It does not calculate ratio from how many children are actually in the room. There's no dedicated ratio calculation from attendance.

**Sam Wood:** So if I set the minimums right, it tells me which room is short a person.

**Alex Chen:** Exactly. It tells you which shift is short against the minimum you set. It doesn't know if four kids went home early.

**Sam Wood:** Okay, that'd still help. Honestly most of my mornings are just "who's missing where".

**Emily Carter:** That's fine for us. The directors know the enrollment; they just need to see the gaps.

**Alex Chen:** Great. Labor forecasting on Pro can use enrollment numbers as an input, so directors get a suggestion for how many people a week needs. But the ratio itself stays with the directors.

**Sam Wood:** Another one — floaters move between centers. Like, I might send someone from Louisville to Superior at seven, then back at noon. Can it handle that?

**Alex Chen:** Yes. A floater can belong to several centers, and you can give them a shift at Superior in the morning and Louisville in the afternoon. Their hours roll up across both, so if they're heading into overtime the director sees it before approving.

**Sam Wood:** Oh, that's huge. That's where all our overtime comes from.

**Emily Carter:** And the teachers — will they find it easy? Some of them aren't very techy.

**Alex Chen:** The app is basically two screens: "my shifts" and "open shifts". And anyone who doesn't install it still gets reminders by text. What usually happens is the younger teachers start using it in week one and show everyone else.

**Emily Carter:** That sounds like our centers. [laughs]

**Alex Chen:** And the cooks — they'd just be another role, with their own template in the kitchen row. They wouldn't see classroom shifts unless you mark them as qualified.

**Emily Carter:** Good. We have some cooks who also have their teaching certificate and help out at lunch.

**Alex Chen:** Then you'd give them both roles, and they'd see both kinds of open shifts.

**Sam Wood:** And can directors message their whole center? Like, "staff meeting moved to Thursday"?

**Alex Chen:** Yes, there's messaging per location. Announcements go to the app, or by text for anyone without it.

**Sam Wood:** Nice. That replaces at least one of my group texts.

**Emily Carter:** Okay. Can you tell me what this would cost? I'll need to explain it to the board.

**Alex Chen:** Of course. Pro is two hundred twenty dollars per location per month, billed yearly. Eleven centers, that's twenty-nine thousand and forty dollars a year. Plus the onboarding package, three thousand one-time, which is required at ten locations and up. That covers setup, importing your staff and two training sessions.

**Emily Carter:** Twenty-nine thousand and forty, plus three thousand.

**Alex Chen:** That's right. So thirty-two thousand and forty in year one, then twenty-nine forty a year after that.

**Emily Carter:** Our board approves anything over twenty-five thousand, so this goes to them. They meet on the seventeenth of November.

**Alex Chen:** Okay. What would help you most for the board?

**Emily Carter:** A clear written offer. And the story — the classroom closures, the directors' time. I want to make sure we get this right.

**Alex Chen:** I can do that. I'll send a written offer by Friday, October thirtieth, Pro for eleven centers at twenty-nine thousand and forty a year, plus three thousand onboarding, so you have it well before the board. Should I copy Monica?

**Emily Carter:** Yes, please copy Monica. She prepares the board packet.

**Sam Wood:** And training — would that be me?

**Alex Chen:** You and the directors, most likely. Our onboarding manager, Marco, runs the two training sessions. We can figure out the details once the board decides.

**Emily Carter:** Good. Okay — send it over, I'll take it to the board on the 17th.

**Alex Chen:** Will do. Offer to you and Monica by Friday the thirtieth. And if questions come up before the board, just email me and we'll find a time to talk.

**Emily Carter:** Thank you, Alex. This was really helpful.

**Sam Wood:** Yeah, thanks. Cool stuff.

**Alex Chen:** Thanks both. Have a good rest of the week.
